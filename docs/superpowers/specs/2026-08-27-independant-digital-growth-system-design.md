# Indépendant Digital growth and monetization system

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Status:** Approved direction, implementation baseline  
**Market:** France  
**Primary domain:** `independantdigital.fr`

## 1. Objective

Build a French editorial and advisory business that helps public organizations and companies reduce strategic dependence on the United States digital ecosystem.

The site must earn trust before it monetizes that trust. It will explain choices, diagnose needs, sell useful educational products and consulting, and introduce organizations to suitable providers. Provider compensation is allowed, but it must be disclosed, non-exclusive, consented, and separated from buyer-side procurement advice.

Success is not raw traffic. Success is a growing base of qualified French visitors who complete a diagnostic, buy a useful product, request advice, or explicitly request an introduction to a suitable provider.

## 2. Positioning

### Brand promise

> Des décisions numériques plus indépendantes, expliquées clairement et adaptées aux contraintes françaises.

The site is an evaluator and guide, not a reseller catalogue. It must be able to recommend:

- a single targeted alternative;
- several providers as parts of an ecosystem;
- a staged coexistence or migration;
- the incumbent solution when changing would create more risk than value;
- no provider when the evidence is insufficient.

### What “independent” means

Independence is operational, not rhetorical:

- recommendation criteria are public;
- commercial relationships are disclosed near the recommendation and form;
- commercial partners receive no automatic ranking;
- providers have no editorial veto beyond factual corrections;
- introductions require explicit, named consent;
- the site may recommend a non-partner or no migration;
- Indépendant Digital does not receive provider compensation for a procurement on which it is paid to advise the buyer.

## 3. French-market ICP

### Primary organization profile

French SMEs, ETIs, regulated organizations, and mid-sized public bodies with a real dependency decision and enough operational complexity to value guidance.

Strong signals include:

- Microsoft 365, Teams, Zoom, Google Workspace, AWS, or another non-European dependency is under review;
- sensitive, health, public, industrial, legal, financial, or strategic data is involved;
- NIS2, GDPR, HDS, SecNumCloud, procurement, or internal security policy affects the choice;
- a contract renewal, incident, merger, audit, or cloud program creates a decision window;
- management wants a staged plan rather than an ideological “rip and replace.”

### Decision roles

The DSI and RSSI are the primary acquisition audience. CTOs matter in technology-led organizations. CEOs and DGs are sponsors or direct buyers in smaller organizations. Public-sector language uses DG, DGS, DGA, DSI, RSSI, procurement, and transformation roles rather than “CEO.”

### Jobs to be done

1. Map current dependencies and risks.
2. Separate sovereignty, security, compliance, resilience, and environmental claims.
3. Identify realistic alternatives for each use case.
4. Compare transition cost, compatibility, operational effort, and reversibility.
5. Build a staged migration or coexistence plan.
6. Find credible providers and start a useful conversation without surrendering contact data indiscriminately.

## 4. Revenue model

### Reader-funded products

- Free diagnostic and introductory guides.
- Low-cost practical guides and checklists.
- Paid white papers with original analysis, decision matrices, and templates.
- A paid course for DSI, RSSI, DG, and transformation teams.
- Workshops and training.

### Buyer-funded services

- Dependency mapping.
- Strategy and migration-roadmap consulting.
- Requirements workshops.
- Provider shortlisting and due diligence.
- Procurement support when the commercial-conflict rules permit it.

### Provider-funded introductions

Providers may pay for an accepted qualified opportunity or another clearly defined commercial event. Raw contact lists are not sold. Introductions are named, purpose-limited, and explicitly requested by the organization.

The commercial model should begin with a 90-day non-exclusive pilot. Pricing is set from expected first-year gross profit, historical close probability, sales effort, and an acquisition-cost ceiling. A fee per accepted qualified opportunity is preferable to a fee per unverified form submission.

## 5. Recommendation and provider model

### Provider evaluation dimensions

Every evaluated provider uses the same dimensions where relevant:

- functional coverage by use case;
- ownership, control, and applicable jurisdictions;
- data locations and subprocessors;
- security architecture and available evidence;
- qualifications and certifications with exact scope and expiry;
- identity, SSO, API, export, and integration support;
- accessibility;
- reversibility and exit formats;
- operating model, support, and continuity;
- pricing model and total transition cost;
- environmental evidence and methodology;
- fit, limitations, and unanswered questions.

“French,” “European,” “sovereign,” “secure,” “compliant,” and “low carbon” are not interchangeable labels. Each requires its own evidence and scope.

### Provider page template

Every provider page contains:

1. What the provider does.
2. Best-fit organizations and use cases.
3. Situations where it does not fit.
4. Capabilities by workload.
5. Hosting, jurisdiction, security, and evidence.
6. Integration and reversibility.
7. Pricing information or a clear statement that pricing is not public.
8. Evidence gaps and questions to ask during a demo.
9. Commercial-relationship disclosure.
10. Alternatives section.
11. Last reviewed date, reviewer, sources, and change log.

### Alternatives rule

An alternatives section is mandatory on every provider or solution page where credible alternatives exist. It should normally contain three to five options selected for different scenarios, not a generic list of competitors.

A standalone alternatives page is created only when all of the following are true:

- search intent is distinct from the parent provider or category page;
- France-specific keyword data shows at least 50 relevant monthly searches, or Search Console shows at least 100 relevant impressions over 90 days;
- the query has commercial or diagnostic value for the primary ICP;
- at least three credible alternatives can be assessed with original analysis;
- the page will not cannibalize a stronger existing page.

The numeric threshold is a starting rule, not a substitute for judgment. A lower-volume query may qualify when opportunity value is high and the intent is unusually specific. When the threshold is not met, the alternatives content stays inside the parent page.

No page may be created solely by changing provider names in a template. Each page must explain why the alternatives differ, who each is for, and what trade-offs change the decision.

## 6. AIFEL partnership

### Status

AIFEL is a non-exclusive commercial partner, not a default recommendation.

### Defensible category

> Outil français de visioconférence et de collaboration sécurisée, différencié par une cartographie immersive 3D des espaces de travail.

AIFEL may be recommended:

- as the secure collaboration layer in a wider independent ecosystem;
- as a targeted alternative to Teams, Zoom, or another collaboration service for a defined population or project;
- for secure meetings, team messaging, file collaboration, events, committees, training spaces, or visually organized project environments, when the verified product scope supports the requirement.

AIFEL should not be presented as a complete replacement for email, desktop office applications, device management, identity, ERP, analytics, or other layers that have not been shown to be in scope.

### AIFEL decision roles

- DSI: integration, governance, service operation, and adoption.
- RSSI: sensitive exchanges, security evidence, jurisdiction, and incident handling.
- CTO: architecture, SSO, APIs, and reversibility.
- CEO or DG: strategic exposure, continuity, cost, and organizational change.
- DGS or public-sector leadership: public constraints, continuity, procurement, and data control.

### Evidence gate

Before publication or lead routing, obtain and review:

- corporate ownership and applicable jurisdictions;
- hosting provider, locations, and subprocessors;
- DPA and transfer mechanisms;
- exact SecNumCloud and HDS claims, certificate holder, scope, and dates;
- encryption and key-management details by module;
- SSO, SAML, SCIM, MFA, APIs, and export formats;
- SLA, support, incident notification, continuity, and disaster recovery;
- accessibility status;
- pricing bands and implementation conditions;
- proof and permission for customer logos, testimonials, and performance claims;
- a product demonstration and hands-on evaluation.

### Carbon claim

French operation and the absence of routine US data transfers may support a sovereignty argument. They do not, by themselves, prove that AIFEL has the lowest carbon impact.

Until a comparative lifecycle assessment exists, acceptable copy is limited to factual architecture and a qualified explanation. “Impact carbone plus bas que tout le monde,” “green,” and equivalent global claims are prohibited.

A comparative claim requires a defined functional unit, equivalent scenario, terminals, networks, data centers, electricity mix, storage, video quality, participant count, period, method, and independent source. The public wording must reproduce the study’s scope and date.

### Referral disclosure and consent

Recommended disclosure:

> AIFEL est un partenaire commercial non exclusif d’Indépendant Digital. Nous pouvons être rémunérés pour certaines mises en relation qualifiées. Ce partenariat n’entraîne aucune recommandation automatique et AIFEL est évalué selon la même méthode que les autres solutions.

Recommended opt-in:

> Je souhaite être mis en relation avec AIFEL pour étudier ce besoin. J’accepte qu’Indépendant Digital transmette à AIFEL mes coordonnées et les informations relatives à mon projet. AIFEL pourra me contacter au sujet de cette demande. Cette mise en relation peut donner lieu à une rémunération d’Indépendant Digital.

The checkbox is unchecked, versioned, timestamped, and separate from newsletter consent. No AIFEL data is transferred when a visitor merely downloads content or completes the independent diagnostic.

## 7. Information architecture and topical authority

### Core hubs

1. **Comprendre**: sovereignty, strategic autonomy, Cloud Act, GDPR transfers, NIS2, SecNumCloud, HDS, procurement, reversibility.
2. **Évaluer**: diagnostic, dependency map, TCO, risk register, requirements templates, migration readiness.
3. **Solutions**: collaboration and video, email, office, file sharing, identity, cloud, backup, cybersecurity, CRM, analytics, and other validated categories.
4. **Fournisseurs**: evidence-backed provider profiles.
5. **Alternatives**: demand-validated alternatives to named incumbents and categories.
6. **Scénarios**: DSI, RSSI, CTO, PME/ETI, public sector, healthcare, regulated and sensitive projects.
7. **Agir**: consulting, guides, white papers, course, workshops, and consented introductions.

### Initial content sequence

The launch sequence favors decision support over volume:

1. Homepage and methodology.
2. Digital-dependency diagnostic.
3. Secure collaboration category guide.
4. AIFEL provider page after evidence review.
5. Alternatives to Microsoft Teams for sensitive French organizations, after keyword validation.
6. Alternatives to Zoom for secure French videoconferencing, after keyword validation.
7. Staged Microsoft 365 dependency-reduction roadmap.
8. Provider-evaluation checklist.
9. Public-sector and regulated-ETI scenario pages.
10. First paid guide derived from real diagnostic questions and sales conversations.

### Internal linking

Every informational article links to one category or scenario hub, one relevant tool or diagnostic, and the next practical action. Provider pages link to their category, relevant scenarios, methodology, and alternatives. Alternatives pages link back to the evaluation method and individual provider pages.

## 8. French-first language policy

French is the only active acquisition and indexation language at launch.

The English templates and database content remain in the repository for future use, but English is removed from primary navigation, excluded from acquisition campaigns, and marked `noindex,follow` until it reaches the same editorial and evidence standard as French. The language switcher is hidden during this period. English content is not deleted.

The site must not publish an English translation automatically. Reactivation requires a separate market decision, manual editorial review, hreflang verification, and parity for disclosures and provider evidence.

## 9. Editorial quality system

### Publication requirements

Every page has:

- a named owner and human reviewer;
- creation and last-reviewed dates;
- a defined search intent and reader decision;
- primary sources next to material claims;
- clear separation of fact, interpretation, and recommendation;
- commercial disclosure when applicable;
- fit, limitations, and alternatives;
- an original element such as a decision matrix, test, interview, data analysis, screenshot, template, or worked scenario;
- a manual mobile, link, and form check.

### Prohibited launch content

- invented testimonials, logos, customer counts, savings, certifications, or market statistics;
- unsupported superlatives or “best” claims;
- fake barometers or original-research labels without a published method and dataset;
- certification language that omits service scope;
- blanket guarantees against foreign law or access;
- generic competitor tables designed to force the partner to win;
- environmental claims without a recognized method and evidence;
- programmatic pages with only names and keywords changed.

### Review cadence

- Laws, official qualifications, security claims, pricing, and provider capabilities: review at least quarterly and after any material update.
- Evergreen guides: review every six months.
- Comparison and alternatives pages: review quarterly.
- Forms, consent copy, disclosures, and routing: test monthly and after every release.

The site maintains a claim register with claim, scope, source, owner, review date, and status. Expired or unverified claims are removed or clearly labelled.

## 10. Lead qualification and routing

### Diagnostic fields

Collect only what is needed to make a recommendation:

- organization type and sector;
- decision role;
- current tools;
- use cases under review;
- sensitivity and regulatory constraints;
- user and external-participant scope;
- hosting or jurisdiction requirements;
- integration and identity requirements;
- timeline and procurement route;
- budget range when appropriate;
- desired next step.

### Routing states

1. **Independent result only**: no provider transfer.
2. **More information required**: request a consultation or clarification.
3. **Suitable provider identified**: explain fit, limitations, and alternatives.
4. **Named introduction requested**: record consent and transfer only the agreed fields.
5. **Conflict or poor fit**: do not route; explain the better next step.

### Qualified AIFEL opportunity

AIFEL receives an opportunity only when secure collaboration or videoconferencing is central, French operation matters, the approximate scope and timing are known, a relevant decision role is involved, and explicit AIFEL consent is recorded.

The partner agreement defines duplicate rules, acceptance and response SLAs, purpose limitation, no onward transfer, retention, status feedback, lost reasons, quality caps, termination, and permitted endorsement wording.

## 11. Marketing plan

### Organic search

Build category authority through strong hubs, provider evidence pages, demand-validated alternatives pages, scenario pages, and diagnostic tools. Search-volume data comes from Google Ads Keyword Planner and a professional SEO dataset when available; Search Console replaces estimates with observed demand after launch.

### Paid search

Start with high-intent French queries around alternatives, audits, migration planning, and secure collaboration. Send each ad group to a page that answers the exact query. Do not send alternatives traffic to a generic homepage.

Use negative keywords to exclude consumer, free-download, crack, personal-use, employment, and support-only intent when those visitors cannot become customers.

### Outbound email

Build narrow lists around trigger events and roles. A useful sequence leads with an observed dependency or decision, links to a relevant diagnostic or evidence page, and offers a short conversation. It does not lead with AIFEL unless the recipient’s use case makes that connection relevant.

### Partner and authority channels

- Joint webinars labelled as partner content.
- Interviews with French providers, practitioners, DSI, RSSI, and public-sector experts.
- Case studies with named participants and permission.
- Professional associations, clusters, chambers, and sector publications.
- A practical newsletter based on material product, qualification, and regulatory changes.

### Initial funnel

Search, outbound, webinar, or referral → use-case page → diagnostic → independent result → guide, consultation, or named provider introduction.

## 12. Measurement

### Acquisition

- qualified organic visits by content cluster;
- paid cost per diagnostic start and completion;
- outbound reply and meeting rates;
- non-brand versus brand search growth.

### Editorial

- pages passing manual review;
- claims with current primary evidence;
- assisted conversions by page;
- return visits, diagnostic starts, and useful-scroll depth;
- corrections and update latency.

### Revenue

- guide and course revenue;
- consulting opportunities and revenue;
- qualified introductions requested;
- partner acceptance, demo, proposal, win, and loss rates;
- revenue and gross contribution per accepted opportunity.

### Trust and compliance

- consent-record completeness;
- complaint and unsubscribe rates;
- provider-routing errors;
- conflicts detected before transfer;
- pages carrying complete disclosure.

## 13. Delivery phases

### Phase 0: credibility and legal foundation

Remove unsupported proof, demo logos, invented metrics, blanket certifications, fake research, and overbroad legal or environmental claims. Establish the brand, domain, methodology, editorial policy, partner disclosure, privacy language, and French-first indexation.

### Phase 1: core conversion system

Publish the homepage, methodology, diagnostic, category architecture, lead-consent model, and analytics events. Make the diagnostic useful without requiring a provider introduction.

### Phase 2: AIFEL and alternatives acquisition

Complete AIFEL due diligence, publish its fit/not-fit page, add the alternatives section, validate Microsoft Teams and Zoom alternative demand, and launch only the pages that pass the threshold.

### Phase 3: authority and monetization

Publish scenario clusters, create the first paid guide and white paper, run the course pilot, and add verified case studies.

### Phase 4: scaled acquisition

Launch tightly structured paid search, outbound sequences, joint webinars, retargeting where consent permits it, and partner expansion based on lead quality rather than volume.

## 14. Launch gates

The site does not launch on `independantdigital.fr` until:

- all visible claims have evidence or appropriately qualified wording;
- demo customers and invented testimonials are removed;
- French navigation, canonical URLs, sitemap, and structured data use the final domain;
- English is parked according to the language policy;
- privacy, cookies, partner disclosure, and named consent are implemented;
- forms work and do not transfer provider data without consent;
- mobile layouts pass at 320, 375, 414, and 768 pixels;
- accessibility, links, metadata, and structured data have been manually checked;
- at least one useful diagnostic path and one non-commercial next step work end to end.
